# Chapter 13

## The Signal That Keeps the System On

### (The Third Craving)

---

### Narration

Most practitioners, once they have worked with craving for a while,
notice something unexpected.

The obvious forms become manageable.

The reaching toward pleasant objects — they can see it now. They can
soften the grip. They can let the object remain without amplifying it.

The pushing away from unpleasant objects — they can see that too.
They can release the resistance. They can let the sensation be present
without bracing against it.

And then something strange happens.

Both of those quiet down.

And the system keeps running.

Not dramatically. Not urgently. Not with any obvious object.

Just... on.

A subtle hum. A background interest. A barely perceptible investment
in the fact that something is happening at all.

This is the third craving.

It does not announce itself.
It has no obvious object.
It does not feel like wanting or not-wanting.

It feels like nothing in particular.

Which is why almost no one sees it.

And why, once you do see it, the deepest settling becomes possible
for the first time.

---

### Dialogue

**Student:**
I've been working with craving the way you described. I can see the
reaching. I can see the resistance. But the system keeps running even
when both of those quiet down. What is that?

**Teacher:**
That is the third craving.

**Student:**
What is it craving?

**Teacher:**
Continuation.

**Student:**
Continuation of what?

**Teacher:**
The process itself.

Not any particular experience.
Not any particular state.
Not any particular outcome.

Just: the fact that something is happening.

---

### The Structure of the Three Cravings

**Teacher:**
You already know the first two.

The first craving is approach gain running hot — the system pulling
toward pleasant formations. Wanting more. Wanting this to continue.
Wanting better.

The second craving is avoidance gain running hot — the system
pushing away from unpleasant formations. Wanting less. Wanting this
to stop. Wanting different.

Both of these have clear objects.
Both have clear directional pull.
Both are workable once they become visible.

The third craving has no object.
No direction.
No pull.

It is the global gain signal — the system maintaining its own
excitation above the threshold required to keep the process going.

**Student:**
So it's not wanting anything specific. It's just wanting to keep
wanting?

**Teacher:**
Closer.

It is the system's investment in its own continuation.

A barely perceptible yes to the fact that experience is occurring.

**Student:**
That sounds harmless.

**Teacher:**
It is not harmful in itself.

But it is what keeps the system running when everything else has
quieted. And it is what prevents the deepest settling from occurring
naturally.

---

### What It Feels Like

**Student:**
How do I recognize it?

**Teacher:**
It is subtle enough that most people cannot see it directly at first.

You recognize it by process of elimination.

When approach gain is not running hot —
when avoidance gain is not running hot —
when the system is neither over nor under-energized —
when volitional seizure is not present —

and the system is still running —

that background running is the continuation signal.

**Student:**
So it's what's left when everything else is absent?

**Teacher:**
Yes.

**Student:**
And what does it actually feel like?

**Teacher:**
Like mild interest without an object.

Like the sense that something should happen next.

Like a very quiet readiness.

Like the system leaning slightly forward — not toward anything
specific — just forward.

**Student:**
I recognize that.

**Teacher:**
Most people do, once it's pointed at.

It has been there the whole time. It simply did not have a name
or a form that made it visible.

---

### Why This One Is Different

**Student:**
Why is the third craving harder to see than the first two?

**Teacher:**
Because the first two have objects.

When you are reaching toward a pleasant sensation, there is a
sensation you can point to. There is a direction you can feel.
The craving has a handle.

When you are resisting an unpleasant sensation, there is a sensation
you can point to. There is a bracing you can feel. That craving also
has a handle.

The third craving has no handle.

It is not toward anything.
It is not away from anything.

It is simply: on.

**Student:**
So it's like the difference between a specific current and the
electricity itself.

**Teacher:**
That is a precise analogy.

The first two cravings are specific currents running in specific
directions.

The third craving is the power being on.

---

### The Algorithm Applied

**Teacher:**
You learned the algorithm in an earlier chapter.

If A causes B, then reducing A reduces B.

Applied here:

The continuation signal sustains the system's running.

Reducing the continuation signal allows the system to settle to
its natural ground state.

Not by suppression.
Not by force.
Not by deciding to stop.

By simply not sustaining the interest.

**Student:**
How do you not sustain interest without suppressing it?

**Teacher:**
This is the subtle point.

Suppression is pushing the interest away.
That is avoidance gain — the second craving.
It feeds the system, not starves it.

Not sustaining is different.

It is neither reaching toward the interest nor pushing it away.

It is simply not adding fuel.

The interest is present.
You do not amplify it.
You do not resist it.
You do not find it particularly interesting.

**Student:**
That sounds circular. Using interest to not be interested.

**Teacher:**
It is paradoxical until you see it directly.

The clearest pointer is this:

You are not trying to make the interest stop.
You are not waiting for the interest to stop.
You are not monitoring whether the interest has stopped.

You are simply not feeding it.

The way you do not feed a fire is not by pouring water on it.
It is by not adding wood.

**Student:**
And then what happens?

**Teacher:**
The system naturally settles.

Not dramatically.
Not suddenly.
Not with any sense of achievement.

It simply... quiets.

The background hum reduces.
The forward lean releases.
The sense of readiness dissolves.

And what remains is the ground state.

---

### The Ground State

**Student:**
What is the ground state?

**Teacher:**
The substrate's own nature, encountered directly.

Not as a concept.
Not as a description.
Not as a state you achieve.

As what is present when the continuation signal is not sustaining
the system above its natural resting point.

**Student:**
What does that feel like?

**Teacher:**
Complete without requiring anything.

Not blissful in an excited sense.
Not empty in a dull sense.

Just: present. Whole. Sufficient.

The traditions describe this as the void, as emptiness, as pure
awareness, as the ground of being.

None of those descriptions are wrong.

But here we describe it functionally:

It is what is present when the three cravings are not operating.

**Student:**
And then the system turns back on?

**Teacher:**
Usually yes.

A sound. A sensation. A thought. Something contacts the system and
the process begins again.

But contact with the ground state — even briefly — changes the
priors. Something is recognized that cannot be unrecognized.

The system keeps running. But it runs differently.

---

### The Practical Question

**Student:**
So what do I actually do with this?

**Teacher:**
First, learn to recognize it.

Use the elimination method.

When the five failure modes are absent and the system is stable and
first jhana conditions are present — look for the continuation
signal.

The subtle forward lean.
The mild interest without object.
The background readiness.

You do not have to do anything with it at first.

Just see it.

Seeing it clearly is the most important step.

**Student:**
And then?

**Teacher:**
Then practice not adding fuel.

Not once. Not in a single session. As a gradual refinement across
many sessions.

Each time you notice the continuation signal and neither reach toward
it nor push it away, the groove of equanimity deepens slightly.

Each time you allow the interest to be present without sustaining it,
the system learns that the continuation signal does not require
action.

Over time, the signal quiets on its own more readily.

**Student:**
How long does this take?

**Teacher:**
It varies significantly.

Some practitioners encounter the ground state relatively quickly once
they can see the continuation signal clearly.

Others work with it for a long time.

The metric is not speed.
It is clarity of recognition.

When the continuation signal is clearly seen — as a distinct
mechanical event, not as a vague sense that something is happening
— the conditions for the deepest settling are present.

Everything else follows from that recognition.

---

### Common Mistakes

**Student:**
What do people usually get wrong here?

**Teacher:**
Three things.

**The first mistake: trying to stop the continuation signal.**

Trying to stop it is the second craving — avoidance gain applied to
the continuation signal itself.

It feeds the system rather than allowing it to settle.

The move is not stopping. It is not sustaining.

**The second mistake: mistaking equanimity for indifference.**

Equanimity is not not caring.
It is not emotional flatness.
It is not detachment from life.

Equanimity is the absence of the three cravings.

The experience field remains vivid. Emotions still arise. Sensation
is still present. Other beings still matter.

What is absent is the automatic amplification — the reaching,
the pushing, the sustaining.

Life does not become less. It becomes less effortful.

**The third mistake: making the ground state a goal.**

The moment the ground state becomes something you are trying to
achieve, the first craving — approach gain — is active.

Which prevents the settling that would allow the ground state to
appear.

The ground state is not a destination.
It is what is present when you stop sustaining the journey.

**Student:**
So the way to get there is to stop trying to get there.

**Teacher:**
The way to allow it to appear is to stop sustaining what is
preventing it from appearing.

Which is not the same as trying to stop trying.

It is simply not adding wood to the fire.

---

### How This Connects to the Deeper Path

**Teacher:**
In the architecture of dependent origination, the three cravings are
the weak link.

They are the point in the chain where the volitional mechanism can
intervene.

The first craving sustains pleasant formations beyond their natural
arc.
The second craving sustains unpleasant formations beyond their
natural arc.
The third craving sustains the entire system beyond its natural
resting point.

Seeing all three clearly — and learning not to sustain them — is
what allows formations to complete their natural arc.

And when formations complete their natural arc without being
sustained, something becomes visible that is always present beneath
the sustaining.

The sensitive ground itself.

Not as a concept.
As direct recognition.

**Student:**
Is that what cessation is?

**Teacher:**
Cessation is the full temporary release of the continuation signal
at the system level.

The mind turns off. Then turns back on. No content in between.

It is not a goal to pursue.
It is what happens when the system is sufficiently refined and the
continuation signal is not being sustained.

If it occurs, it occurs.

What matters more for daily practice is the gradual recognition of
the continuation signal and the gradual refinement of not sustaining
it.

That is what changes the priors.
That is what changes how the system runs over time.
That is what makes the path self-correcting.

---

### Closing Narration

There are three cravings.

You now know all three.

The first: reaching toward pleasant formations.
The second: resisting unpleasant formations.
The third: sustaining the system's own continuation.

The first two have objects and handles.
The third has neither.

The first two feel like something.
The third feels like background noise you stopped noticing.

All three sustain formations beyond their natural arc.
All three respond to the same basic move: not adding fuel.

When all three are seen clearly — and when the instrument is refined
enough to not sustain them habitually — the system settles to its
natural ground state.

Not once.
Not permanently.
Not dramatically.

Gradually. Repeatedly. Quietly.

Each settling changes the priors slightly.
Each recognition deepens the groove of equanimity.
Each moment of not sustaining is the path in action.

This is not exotic.
This is not reserved for advanced practitioners.
This is the ordinary work of a system learning to run without
unnecessary fuel.

You have the map.
You have the diagnostic tools.
You have the correctives.

Now it is simply a matter of looking carefully at your own experience
and noticing how the system behaves.

That is all this ever was.
